3. What the Market Is Trading

The chip chain is being de-levered, not merely repriced. $INTC collapsed 28.58% on Friday and 44.89% over 20 days. $AMD fell 10.88% for the session and 20.37% over 20 days, while $ARM lost 9.97% Friday and 33.02% over the same period. The damage spread across the full stack: $TSM fell 5.95%, $ASML dropped 6.55%, and $TER declined 7.38%. That breadth points to crowded exposure being unwound across compute, foundry, equipment, and test—not a single-company earnings problem.

AI infrastructure is splitting between backlog credibility and financing risk. $VRT gained 19.65% over five sessions despite falling 5.46% Friday, reflecting continued enthusiasm around data-center power and cooling backlogs. By contrast, $CRWV lost 38.77% over 20 days, while $DELL fell 24.57% and $MRVL dropped 27.87%. Financing news around a proposed $2.4 billion AI data-center loan reinforces the deeper issue: demand remains large, but the market is placing a higher discount on capital intensity, funding availability, delivery schedules, and the path from bookings to free cash flow.

Software is no longer receiving automatic credit for AI commentary. $NOW fell 33.87% over five days even as research commentary acknowledged improving AI traction. $NET lost 13.82%, $CRM declined 7.80%, and $SNOW fell 5.95%. The market is demanding evidence that AI features can lift revenue growth rather than simply sustain spending. Narrative momentum without visible operating leverage is being faded.

Defensive growth is taking the other side of the trade. $LLY advanced 11.31% for the week and 24.04% over 20 days, while $ABBV gained 12.20% over 20 days. In Staples, $WMT rose 18.46% over 20 days. Energy showed similar sponsorship, with $COP up 21.52%, $XOM up 14.17%, and $CVX up 13.95%. These are not classic panic trades into low-volatility assets alone; flows are clustering around earnings durability, pricing power, and current cash generation.

Mega-cap communication platforms are losing their defensive halo. $GOOGL fell 12.61% over five days and 18.53% over 20 days, while $META dropped 15.53% over 20 days. Even strong cloud-growth narratives are being weighed against AI capex, slower advertising or engagement trends, and a higher bar for incremental returns. The exception was $TMUS, up 6.21% Friday and 6.76% for the week, reflecting demand for recurring revenue and lower-duration cash flows.

6. Institutions & Research

AI capex: sustainability is now the question. Goldman Sachs’ risk-appetite work points to technology dominating cross-asset moves as sentiment slips below neutral. That framing matches the price action: the market is no longer questioning whether AI spending will remain large, but whether returns, financing, and free cash flow can keep pace with the buildout. Morgan Stanley’s data-center capacity model sees roughly 20% compound annual growth to more than 300 GW by 2030, with $EQIX highlighted as a key beneficiary of constrained capacity.

Cloud strength remains visible, but operating leverage carries more weight. Morgan Stanley sees $MSFT benefiting from faster growth and durable operating leverage, with a 650 target, while Citi points to Azure and Copilot momentum and carries a 636 target. The market’s reaction function is nevertheless stricter: strong cloud demand alone is no longer enough if capex intensity rises or incremental margins fail to expand.

Software AI monetization is separating from product adoption. Morgan Stanley argues that improving AI traction at $NOW has not yet produced faster headline growth, leading to a lower 160 target. The stock’s 33.87% five-day drawdown shows how aggressively the tape is discounting that gap. For $PLTR, Morgan Stanley’s channel work indicates the possibility of a 13th consecutive quarter of growth acceleration, but the valuation debate remains embedded in its Equal-weight stance and 205 target.

Semiconductor fundamentals and semiconductor equities are trading on different clocks. JPMorgan sees AI-led test demand supporting $TER, with attention shifting toward the 2027 ramp and the durability of memory demand. Citi also sees share gains remaining on track. UBS and Morgan Stanley remain constructive on leading Asian memory producers, citing strong execution, earnings revisions, and shareholder returns. Yet US-listed semiconductor prices are trading near-term crowding and liquidity first, with Teradyne down 38.90% over 20 days.

Internet platforms are being judged on growth quality. Goldman analysts remain constructive on $GOOGL, while Citi highlights accelerating cloud growth even as search and YouTube growth moderate. Morgan Stanley’s work similarly focuses on the hunt for incremental growth. The stock’s 18.53% decline over 20 days indicates that investors are balancing cloud acceleration against AI investment, platform disruption, and slower core-business momentum.

Power, cooling, and connectivity retain stronger backlog visibility. Citi argues that $VRT is benefiting from an accelerating global project pipeline and raises its target to 362. Citi also sees durable execution and growth at $APH, supported by AI connectivity demand. Goldman’s and Citi’s work across advanced PCB and copper-clad laminate suppliers points to tightening supply, higher AI-product pricing, and margin expansion, suggesting that selected picks-and-shovels remain fundamentally firmer than the broad technology tape.
