Inference is becoming the next leg of the spending flywheel. Citi analysts argue that inference can create a self-reinforcing cycle in which better models drive usage, usage drives compute demand, and expanding compute capacity enables more capable applications. The market implication is a potential broadening from training clusters into networking, memory, custom silicon, and power infrastructure, although the −16.33% drawdown in $AVGO shows that investors are still debating which suppliers capture that economics.

Critical supply chains are moving closer to the center of valuation. JPMorgan argues that substrates, printed circuit boards, and other bottleneck components matter more as AI systems become denser and more complex. BofA’s supply-chain work similarly points to improving visibility into 2027, even where near-term profitability has been uneven. This supports the idea that the next AI trade may be less about headline chip volumes and more about yield, packaging, interconnect, and capacity constraints.

Internet platforms are being judged on monetization divergence. Morgan Stanley’s platform analysis highlights Meta’s improving strategic position, while Goldman analysts frame e-commerce as increasingly agentic. Bernstein’s work on digital advertising also raises the competitive question of when Meta’s growth trajectory could challenge Google Search more directly. Physical AI is extending the opportunity set beyond data centers. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley both examine humanoids and logistics automation as long-duration beneficiaries of AI progress. Their common analytical thread is that software intelligence alone is insufficient: sensor stacks, actuators, power systems, edge compute, and manufacturing scale determine the pace of commercialization. The theme remains early, but it is increasingly connected to the same semiconductor and infrastructure supply chains driving current AI spending.

Enterprise software faces a tougher proof-of-value phase. Deutsche Bank’s discussions across Microsoft, Oracle, ServiceNow, Salesforce, and semiconductor suppliers highlight broad enterprise engagement with AI. Bernstein also sees AI-driven hiring as a potential support for seat-based software demand. Even so, $MSFT’s −2.69% five-day performance shows that the market wants evidence of revenue conversion and margin durability alongside accelerating infrastructure investment.
