1. Market Snapshot

US equities closed the week in the green, but beneath the index surface the tape was a study in extreme dispersion — this was a week where earnings re-priced the Mega-Cap 7 one name at a time.

  • Indices turned up on the week: Nasdaq 25,913.90 (+3.94% wk, +2.13% day), S&P 500 7,600.50 (+2.53% wk, +1.48% day), Dow Jones 53,178.41 (+1.85% wk, +1.32% day). Risk appetite repaired after the late-July "Fed triple-dissent + Middle East oil spike" scare, carried by Big Tech earnings.
  • Microsoft and Amazon were the twin engines: MSFT 487.65 (+25.33% wk, +26.09% 20d) — the "AI finally makes money" narrative off Azure's cloud acceleration; AMZN 284.02 (+22.75% wk, +16.33% 20d) — AWS re-acceleration easing "AI payback" doubts. GOOGL 373.51 (+14.38% wk) rode cloud + search.
  • Apple was the lone guide-down casualty: AAPL 303.42 (−9.94% wk, −2.96% 20d) — top-line beat but soft current-quarter guide and a "once-in-a-century supply gap" warning overshadowed the print; the stock shed nearly 10% on the week.
  • Tesla remains the weakest link: TSLA 322.08 (+4.16% wk but −23.27% 20d) — this week's bounce looks like a dead-cat bounce, not a trend turn. AMD 484.64 (−12.21% 20d) lagged the semis, diverging from NVDA (+5.67% 20d).

One-line summary: the market stopped asking "is AI a bubble" and started asking, name by name, "when does your cloud and cash flow turn into profit." Microsoft, Amazon and Google answered with delivery; Apple, Meta and Tesla are still being grilled.

Theme 5: Tesla's imbalance unresolved. TSLA −23.27% 20d is the deepest drawdown among the Seven. The market keeps pricing the gap between "big bets (Optimus / Cybercab / energy)" and "profit delivery"; the bounce lacks a fresh catalyst.
